Manufacturer, not a trading company

Five steps happen under our roof

Buyers ask the same question in different words: is this assembled from bought-in parts, or built here? A patient lift transfer chair is a mix — tube, castors, actuators and fasteners are components — and what matters is which steps happen in-house and which are inspected on arrival.

1

Cutting and bending. High-carbon steel tube cut to length, bent and drilled on jigs, not freehand.

2

Welding. Joints welded in fixture, then dressed. Visual check on every frame, destructive check on samples.

3

Surface finishing. Degrease, phosphate, powder coat, cure. Colour matched to a physical sample.

4

Assembly. Castors, lift mechanism, seat, backrest and commode bucket fitted; brake and travel measured.

5

Load and function test. Static load at rating, lift cycle test, and a full up-down cycle under load before packing.

Before you order

Five documents you can ask for

If a supplier cannot produce these, the price on the quotation is not comparable to ours — it is a different product with less testing behind it.

Dimensional drawing

Frame width, seat travel and turning radius with tolerances — not just a marketing spec table.

Static load-test report

150 kg held for a stated time, with the rig described so your engineer can judge the method.

Registration and listing

Establishment registration and device listing references, so you can check them yourself.

Incoming inspection standard

What we check on bought-in castors, actuators and gas struts, and what fails a batch.

Packaging test summary

Drop-test results and carton specification — a chair that arrives bent is a return, not a delivery.

On certifications, including what we do not have

EMPCaring is FDA registered, and the models are device-listed. We are not FDA approved or FDA certified, because the FDA does not issue certifications for this device class — any supplier telling you otherwise is either confused or hoping you are.

We also do not hold ISO 13485 for these models, and we will not present a supplier's certificate as our own. If a tender requires it, tell us and we will scope what qualification would actually involve. What we can document today: registration and listing references, load-test reports, dimensional drawings, incoming inspection records and packaging test summaries. For what US buyers must hold to resell this equipment, see DME licensing and Medicare enrollment.
